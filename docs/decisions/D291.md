## D291. Switching from Go's -exec to lsr accepts a replication gap while the first copy uploads

Status: decided · Decided by: the owner, 2026-11-26 · Recorded by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5) · documentation only (`docs/running.md`)

- **Question:** when the application runs as Go's `-exec` child, Go's shutdown closes its last connection, SQLite checkpoints and deletes the WAL, and lsr's first start finds nothing to continue from. It stages a whole-database copy and a first snapshot, and new commits do not reach the replica until the copy is uploaded (about 4 minutes for 24 GiB on a real provider). A 393-line change would have overlapped the copy with new commits to shorten that gap. Should it land?
- **Choice:** no, the change was dropped. The gap is accepted and documented in `docs/running.md`, with the way to avoid it: stop the application outside Go before stopping Go, so the WAL survives and lsr continues from Go's position.
- **Why:** the copy cannot be avoided without a checksum that Go never writes into its files, so lsr cannot vouch for the replica's tail. The overlap would have saved about 74 s, once per switch, for about 393 lines of production code.
- **Cost accepted:** one gap of replication per switch, scaled by database size, and about twice the database's size in staging disk.
- **Revisit if:** Go starts writing a checksum lsr can verify, or switches become frequent.
- **Links:** `docs/running.md` section "Moving from Go Litestream, and back".
