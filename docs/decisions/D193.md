## D193. A maintenance pass lists only what its decision reads, and the whole replica every 15 minutes

Status: decided (the design, by the owner, 2026-11-24) · Decided by: owner; the 15-minute interval, the 120-second retention check and the carried listing are Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5), delegated · Recorded by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5)

- **Question:** every 15-second check and every compaction window listed every level in full, so a replica with 20,000 level-1 files cost about 25,000 LIST requests and about 20 million entries an hour while uploads continued ([#324](https://github.com/emschwartz/litestream-rs/issues/324)). How does the engine ask the store for less without a shorter history ever being decided on?
- **Choice:** the engine keeps, in memory only, the newest file it knows at each level 1 to 8 (about eight small records), learned from whole listings and from its own confirmed compactions. A pass lists only what its decision reads: level 0 whole; a compaction level's own tail (after the newest file known there) and the level below it (after that level's last transaction), only while that level's window is open and it has sources; level 1 after a cover cursor and level 9 whole when level-0 retention is due. A pass with nothing due lists nothing. The pass after this engine's own compaction reuses that pass's listing (at most five seconds old) instead of listing again. A level whose window one of its own outputs closed is quiet until the next window; level-0 retention is checked every 120 seconds (8 check intervals). A whole listing runs at boot, after any fault, refused upload or step back of the clock, after a compaction that found an input gone, before a snapshot job or a delete at level 1 to 8 or of a snapshot, when a retention wake comes, and every 15 minutes as the backstop. The records are dropped on any fault, stop or restart; nothing is written to disk.
- **Why:** it fixes the cost at its cause (the request count and size no longer grow with the history) and keeps no new state on disk, so a restart or fault can only cost one whole listing. A short pass deletes only level-0 files, and its deletes are a subset of what the whole rules would delete from the same replica: its anchor reads the newest known file of every level 1 to 8 (a remembered file is read for its age only, never as a cover), and its floor, cover and stop rules each keep more. The retention gate is read from the last whole listing, at most 15 minutes old. The argument and the per-delete-kind table are in `crates/core/src/maintain/short.rs`; `maintain/retain/tests/short_view.rs` checks the subset property over random replicas, including a level 1 to 8 newest file younger than the last level-0 file, a stale gate and overlapping files, and holds the reviewed nine-file case that broke the first version.
- **Measured** (one simulated hour of one commit a second, `actor/tests/listing.rs`): at 2,900 level-1 files, 10,773 requests and 2,952,201 entries before, 511 and 143,699 after (about 0.37 million requests a month, from about 7.9 million). At 20,000 level-1 files, about 25,000 requests before (too slow to finish in the dev profile; entries scaled from the 2,900 run are about 20 million), 580 requests and 212,099 entries after (about 0.42 million a month, from about 18.3 million). The gated tests fail above 600 requests and 230,000 entries an hour at both sizes. A first version that only narrowed the listings made 3,161 and 3,230 requests; the rest came from not listing when nothing is due, carrying the listing across our own job, and quiet windows.
- **What the second-writer check sees, and when.** Level 0 is listed whole on every retention check (every 120 seconds, was 15) and by every compaction pass of level 1 or when level 1 has sources; an upload another writer's file holds is refused at once, as before. A foreign file at a compaction level above the newest file known there is seen by the next pass that lists that level (its next window). A foreign file below the newest known file of its level, a foreign snapshot (level 9 is listed only when the snapshot window opens or retention is due, was every pass), a foreign file at a level above the configured top, and a replica that lost files below the tail are seen only at the whole listing, at most 15 minutes. **Gap accepted:** levels above the configured top (levels 3 to 8 with the default top of 3) are never listed by a short pass; only the whole listing sees them, and the anchor reads the remembered newest file of each, so a foreign file there is noticed up to 15 minutes late and a remembered record of one that is gone is dropped only by the next whole listing. A restore reads the whole replica and never depends on a short pass.
- **Cost accepted:** the delay above, for a writer that uploads nothing at those levels. A second writer that uploads at level 0 is still seen at the retention check (120 seconds at most) and stops at its next upload. The intervals are entries-versus-delay trades: a whole listing at 20,000 files is about 21 requests and 20,000 entries, so 15 minutes adds about 84 requests and 80,000 entries an hour; the 120-second retention check (against 60 seconds) saved about 52 requests an hour at 20,000 files, which is what got that size from 632 to 580. The alternative, a 30-minute whole listing, would have saved about 40 requests and weakened the check for foreign compaction outputs further, so it was not taken.
- **Revisit if:** a store charges per entry returned rather than per request, which would favour a longer interval, or a user needs another writer noticed faster, which would make the intervals settings.
