## D165. The simulator refuses S3 requests and judges which refusal the engine reports (delegated)

Status: open to owner review · Decided by: Claude (sim-s3-reach), **delegated**

- **Options:** leave refusals to the scenarios and store-s3's classifier tests; draw refusals on the S3 replica and judge only that the engine failed; draw them and judge the kind reported against the simulator's own table of AWS's codes.
- **Choice:** the last. `Op::HttpFault` can now answer one request with a refusal as AWS sends it (`HttpFault::Denied`): 403 `AccessDenied`, `AllAccessDisabled`, `InvalidAccessKeyId`, `SignatureDoesNotMatch` or `RequestTimeTooSkewed`, 400 `ExpiredToken`, `KMS.DisabledException` or `KMS.NotFoundException`, a bare 401, 404 `NoSuchBucket`. A HEAD's refusal carries only its status (403 or 401), since a HEAD has no body. `HttpFault::DenyDeletes` makes the bucket refuse every key of the next batch deletes inside a 200 answer, as object lock or a delete-denying policy does, until the faults heal. A new judge (`DenialMisreported`) takes the refusals met inside each tick and requires one of them among the engine's degraded causes or its stop cause, as the kind `remote::expected_denial` names: a KMS key is the bucket owner's to fix, so `Permission`.
- **Why:** with the replica on S3 no draw refused a request, and no judge told denial kinds apart, so a classifier that sent operators to fix the wrong thing passed every run (D124's measured row). The judge's table is kept apart from `lsr-store-s3`'s classifier so a misreading there is not agreed with.
- **Where the judge stands aside:** another fault met in the same tick (object-level, staging, or a non-refusal provider fault), since the engine reports the first failure a step meets and which request fails first is not the judge's to pin; a crash in the tick; a HEAD whose refusal the tick did not report at all, since it may be a created-at time read ahead, whose failure is dropped by design (`lsr_core::created_at`). It never accepts a wrong kind. Refusals met by the simulator's own reads between ticks are not counted. No refusal is drawn on a whole DeleteObjects: the store's best-effort cleanup of an aborted upload rightly swallows it, and the per-key draw covers retention deletes.
- **Measured:** over 500 swarm seeds at 120 steps with provider faults at weight 40, the judge weighed 512 refusals and stood aside for 22, with no false report (the seeds that fail fail the same way on `MemStore`, but seed 217, below). Unaided recall, 200 mixed seeds each from 1000001, replica on S3, provider faults at weight 40: clean tree 0 failed; `denial-classes` 172; `kms-codes-by-status` 127; `delete-denial-transient` 2 (seeds 1000033 and 1000080; a retention delete is rare in 300 steps). D124 measured 0 for all three. The explicit `sim` test `remote_s3::a_refused_upload_is_reported_as_what_the_operator_must_fix` fails under `denial-classes` and under `kms-codes-by-status`.
- **Cost accepted:** a whole DeleteObjects refused is not judged; a refusal masked by another fault in the same tick is not judged (22 of 534 above); a lasting refusal is not drawn (a fault fires once). Swarm seed 217 on S3 fails liveness (`HealthBlind` on a store-down upload hold), on the branch before this change too; it passes on `MemStore` and is reported, not excused here. Multipart shapes are still not drawn: the simulator's pages are a constant fill, so even a large file compresses far under the 5 MiB part, and drawing one would need incompressible pages in every history.
- **Revisit if:** D79 lands (a 501 to `If-None-Match` becomes a refusal the judge can expect); a lasting refusal draw is wanted (the judge then needs the degraded state to persist, not one report); the delete plant needs better reach (a draw that brings retention deletes forward).
