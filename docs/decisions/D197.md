## D197. A restart dates the lag it finds from the database's last write

Status: decided, delegated · Decided by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5), for the owner, 2026-11-24; the owner has not seen it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5) · Narrows the cost accepted in D174

- **Question:** D174 left a crash loop invisible when a fault stops anything being staged (a full local disk): no staged file exists to date the commit, so each process dates it from its own first sight, the age gauge restarts from zero with every process, and nothing reports it (issue #331). Go has no health endpoint, so it has nothing to copy. What can health report truthfully from what a restart leaves, with no remembered state of its own?
- **Choice (delegated):** the replicator thread reads the database's last modified time (`WalSource::modified`: the later of the database file's and the WAL's, the WAL's only while it holds a frame) with its first reading after the engine has booted, and dates the age it reports (`unreplicated_since`, `lsr_unreplicated_age_seconds`) from that time when the engine finds WAL bytes past the synced position. No commit is newer than the last write, so the age never overstates. Nothing is written anywhere. Lag that appears later in the run is dated by its first sight, as before. Only health's reading changes: the engine's own clock, and with it fail-stop, is untouched.
- **Result:** a process restarted every few seconds over a staging directory that takes no file reports an age that grows across the restarts, from the database's last write, so an alert on `lsr_unreplicated_age_seconds` fires. Test: replica `failstop::a_crash_loop_over_a_staging_disk_that_takes_nothing_reports_a_growing_age`.
- **Cost accepted:** the date is the newest commit's, not the oldest's, so the age is a lower bound. A database written steadily through the loop keeps its last write recent, and the gauge stays low. `/healthz` and fail-stop do not trip for a loop: the engine's clock dates from its own first sight, as D174 left it. Dating that clock the same way was tried and rejected: any lag evidence the engine cannot confirm (frames past the position that the replica may already hold, as after a restarted WAL) became old at once, and the simulator's false-trip judge then failed 12 runs of a 300-second soak (base seed 424242) that the base commit passed. Dating from the replica's newest file, tried first, overstated the age after any idle gap and failed 157 runs of the same soak with false trips (and its extra listing broke the simulator's listing-call limit).
- **Still not covered:** a restart whose only unreplicated commits are in the database file with the WAL gone (a checkpoint while the engine was down): there are no WAL bytes past the position, so no age counts (D81). Retry counts and degraded states do not survive a restart (D174).
- **Revisit if:** the owner accepts a record that survives a restart (then the engine's clock could carry the date and trip), or a deployment needs `/healthz` to fail for the loop.
