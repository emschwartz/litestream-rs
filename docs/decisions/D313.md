## D313. A part that fails is sent again by its own upload, eight tries, waits capped at one second (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5)

- **Question:** during a 24 GiB snapshot upload, one transient error on one 100 MB part failed the whole upload, and the snapshot started over after the engine's backoff (2 min 39 s lost in the full-size drill, and the read transaction and WAL growth restarted). Should a part be retried on its own?
- **What Go does:** the AWS SDK's uploader sends each part through the client's retryer, which litestream sets to 8 attempts per request with waits of at most one second (`.upstream/litestream/s3/replica_client.go`, `newTransportRetryer`). It retries 5xx, throttles, 408, 429 and dropped connections. A part that fails 8 times fails the upload.
- **Decision:** the thread that sends a part sends it again on a failure the engine's own policy calls retryable (`lsr_core::classify`: `Transient`, `Throttled`), up to 8 tries in all. The wait is 100 ms doubling to 1 s, no jitter; a wait the store names (`Retry-After`) is honored up to the 1 s cap and a longer one ends the retries, as `Retrying` does. The parts already sent stay. A part that fails on its last try is the upload's failure with the error it last got, and the caller restarts the upload as before, so a persistent failure still reaches the health handle.
- **Bytes:** a part written from a source is sent again from the same verified cells (what was first seen, or the source read again and checked against it), so the retry carries the bytes the first try signed. A source that gives nothing is the source's failure and is never retried here. A buffered part (`Upload::write`) is held until its request succeeds, as the width budget already counts (D232): the request borrows the buffer through a shared body and gives it back, so a retry sends the same bytes, hashed before the first try, with no copy.
- **Waiting:** a new `Transport::pause`, a no-op by default, so the in-memory and fault transports and the simulator stay deterministic; the socket clients sleep. The store holds no `Sleeper`.
- **Requests:** one extra UploadPart per failed try, as the SDK sends; a clean upload sends none.
- **Cost accepted:** a lane sleeps up to 1 s between tries; waits are not jittered, so lanes that fail together retry together (at most the upload's concurrency, five by default).
- **Revisit if:** a provider needs longer waits than one second to recover (Go's choice, kept).
