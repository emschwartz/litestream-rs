## D295. One compaction takes as many inputs as its read budget holds at the smallest read (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5)

Amends the fan-in of 32 that D199, D205, D212 and D242 state.

- **Question:** with a 60-second level-1 interval and about one level-0 file a second, a level-1 window holds about 60 files. One compaction took at most 32, so each window became two level-1 files and the level-2 merge read two files back per minute where Go reads one: 118 to 120 GetObject an hour against Go's 58 to 60 on the studio head-to-head. What should bound a compaction's inputs?
- **What Go does:** `compactor.go` `Compact` lists the source level from the destination's newest transaction and hands every file to one `ltx.NewCompactor`, one open reader each (upstream #1467). No bound: a backlog of any length is one merge holding every reader at once. That is the defect a bound guards against; a window's worth of files in one merge is not.
- **Why there was a cap:** memory. A merge holds every input at once: its read window, the page waiting to be merged, the decoder's page and frame, and on S3 the GET's connection (each input is one streamed GET, held across the merge). The windows are a role's budget shared out (staging 4 MiB, replica 16 MiB, D212), so the cap is how far a share may shrink.
- **Choice:** the cap is the staging budget, the smaller one, in reads of the smallest size: 4 MiB / 32 KiB = 128 inputs (`MAX_INPUTS`, `crates/core/src/compact.rs`; `SMALLEST_WINDOW`, `crates/core/src/fault/role.rs`). A merge of any allowed fan-in reads within its role's budget: a staged input's window is its share, from 128 KiB at 32 inputs or fewer (unchanged) down to 32 KiB at 128; a replica input's is at least 128 KiB (16 MiB / 128), so the replica reads as before up to 32 inputs and in 128 KiB or more above. The decoder takes any chunk size, so a 64 KiB page arrives over two reads; a staged read is a system call against the page cache, never a request.
- **Measured peak live heap (counting allocator):**
  - Staged, level 1 (`crates/store-fs/tests/store-fs/memory.rs`, `a_full_level_one_compaction_holds_the_staging_budget`): 5.2 MiB at 64 inputs, 6.0 MiB at 128 (at 32: about 5 MiB, D212).
  - Replica read as a directory (`a_compaction_holds_its_share_of_the_budget_per_replica_input`): 17.2 MiB at 64, 18.0 MiB at 128.
  - Replica over S3 through the real client (`crates/store-s3/tests/store-s3/memory.rs`, `a_compaction_from_s3_holds_its_budget_and_a_connection_per_input`): 27.4 MiB at 64, 38.5 MiB at 128, of which the connections are about 165 KiB an input.
  - In-process (`compact::tests::a_full_staged_merge_holds_the_staging_budget`): the buffered bytes stay within the 4 MiB budget plus a page an input at 64 and 128.
- **Counted:** `actor::tests::steady::a_minute_of_level_zero_files_is_one_level_one_file` (a 60-second level 1, 20 commits a second, ten minutes after six): level-1 PUT 20 and level-1 GET 20 before, 10 and 10 after, one a window as Go.
- **Studio (M1 Max, MinIO, a large part size with ten parts in flight and a 60-second level-1 interval, 20 commits a second, against Go 041a8c3):** a 15-minute window after the first snapshot, text-like 4 GiB database. GetObject an hour: 120 before, 60 after, Go 64. Total requests an hour: 4031 before, 3911 after, Go 4351. Steady memory 45 MiB median (54 MiB window peak) before and after, Go 669 MiB; CPU 30 s an hour before and after, Go 155 s. The restored replica held the last commit in each run.
- **Simulator bound:** a first slice that opens every input makes a request each, so the simulator allows 320 requests a tick beyond the files it puts (`BASE_REQUESTS`, `crates/sim/src/cost.rs`), two requests for each of 128 inputs and the listings (a merge of 126 inputs from the replica made 263 in the swarm); it was 128.
- **Cost accepted:** a merge of more than 32 replica inputs over S3 holds about 165 KiB a connection on top of the 16 MiB budget: 39 MiB at 128 where four merges of 32 each held about 22. Such a merge happens only when a level above 1 has a backlog (a steady writer's merges above level 1 take 5 to 12 inputs) or when level 1 is built from the replica because the staged copies are gone. A full staged merge makes up to four times the read system calls of one at 32 KiB, which no gate shows as time.
- **Options not taken:** a staging budget of 16 MiB at 128 KiB reads (four times the staged memory for nothing a page-cache read needs); a cap of 64 (fits a 60-second window but splits again at two minutes); separate caps per role (a level-1 job is planned before the engine knows whether it reads staging or the replica); counting the connection into the replica's budget (a 16 MiB replica budget would then cap a replica merge near 55, and a level-1 job read from the replica would split again).
- **Revisit if:** a level-1 interval above two minutes at one file a second is common, or a profile shows the per-connection buffers of a wide replica merge matter.
