## D254. A level-9 snapshot goes to the replica a slice a tick, with syncs and uploads between (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5) · The step of D217's design for level-9 snapshots (issue #429, from the review in #364)

- **Question:** a level-9 snapshot read from the database was one unit of work. At 50 GiB and 100 MB a second it held the engine's one thread for about 9 minutes, and no commit was synced or uploaded meanwhile. How does the engine keep replicating while it writes one?
- **What Go does, and why:** `DB.SnapshotReader` (`db.go:2877-2960`) takes the sync executor only to read the position and the synced WAL end, then holds the checkpoint lock (`chkMu`, shared) for the length of the copy and lets the executor go. `DB.monitor` keeps syncing (its `sync` takes `chkMu` shared too) and `Replica.monitor` keeps uploading level 0; every checkpoint, the emergency truncate included, skips while the snapshot holds the lock (`checkpointWithExecutor`'s `TryLock`). Go's whole-database copy at level 0 is the sync itself, under the executor, so Go syncs nothing while it stages one. Both are followed here.
- **Choice:**
  1. The snapshot job begins the file (its page map up to the proof's synced end, the header, the upload) and keeps it in memory between ticks (`snapshot::Snapshotting`). Each tick hands the replica at most the tick's bytes (`UploadConfig::bytes_per_tick`, 8 MiB, the budget a staged file's slices have). It publishes only from the slice that handed over the last byte and found the upload's parts answered (`docs/decisions/D233.md`).
  2. Its slices go first, and after every eighth the rest of the engine has a tick, as after a staged file's slices (D217). A commit made during the snapshot is synced and uploaded before the snapshot is whole.
  3. No checkpoint of the engine's runs while it is part-way, as Go's `chkMu`. The read transaction the proof was taken under is then held throughout, and SQLite copies no frame past its read mark, which is at or below the synced end, into the database file: every page newer in the file is a frame the page map holds, and the map wins.
  4. Every slice first reads the wal-index again, and so does the slice that commits, after its last byte: another hold of the read transaction is `ReadTxLost`, another WAL generation or a backfill past the synced end is `WalMoved`, and the snapshot ends with its upload aborted. The job is retried from its first page on its schedule.
  5. The page checksum table is pinned when the snapshot begins (`PageChecksumTable::pin`): the syncs between slices record pages the database file does not hold yet, so a database-file page is checked against the table as it stood then. The pin keeps one slot for each page changed past the snapshot's place, and the chunks a shrink cut. A step that empties the table lets the pin go and ends the snapshot.
  6. A slice whose upload waits for its parts hands nothing over and takes no turn; the tick goes on to other work, and the next tick's slice asks again, as a staged file's send does (D233). The simulator stamps a snapshot's upload when it begins, since the snapshot is stamped then and sent over many ticks, and a boot's settle budget does not count its slices.
  7. A drain runs no maintenance, so it ends the snapshot and aborts its upload; a stop, a rebuild and a crash drop it, and the next start clears what it left. The snapshot a `replicate -once -force-snapshot` drain ends with is written whole in one call, as before.
  8. The level-0 copy of the whole database stays one unit, as Go's. Staging later files during it would not get a commit to the replica sooner (they upload after it) and would leave staged files above a gap after a crash.
- **Cost accepted:**
  - The engine's checkpoints wait for the snapshot, as Go's do, and the WAL grows for its length.
  - Memory is a sum where it was a maximum: the snapshot's page map, its encoder's index (about nine bytes a page) and the replica upload's parts (at most 256 MiB, D232) are held between ticks beside a sync's and a send's. The pin adds 8 bytes for each page changed during the snapshot.
  - A slice reads the wal-index once more and opens the WAL again.
  - 377 production lines (`just prod-lines`).
- **Measured** (Mac Studio, macOS arm64, internal disk, directory replica; main at 16501403b against this branch with it merged, release builds; a 4 GiB database under 20 commits a second of 16 KiB, the scheduled snapshot read from the database with `snapshot.interval` at 1 minute, three runs each, arms interleaved; another session's 20-transaction-a-second drill ran on the machine throughout; the script is not in the repository):
  - The longest stretch with no level-0 upload, from the first snapshot to 3 s after the scheduled one: 2.41 to 2.43 s on main, 1.09 to 1.10 s here, which is the one-second poll interval (the median gap is 1.01 s in both).
  - The snapshot takes 2.39 to 2.40 s on main and 2.33 to 2.36 s here, in 509 slices; two level-0 files go up during it here and none on main. No checkpoint runs during it in either, and the WAL peaks at 3.8 to 4.6 MB in both.
- **Options considered:** slicing the level-0 copy too (above, and not what Go does); a resumable copy of the staged-file send machinery (a level-9 snapshot has no staged file, and staging one would be a local file Go does not write); cancelling the snapshot at a shrink instead of keeping the cut slots (a database with `auto_vacuum=FULL` shrinks at most commits and would rarely finish a snapshot); copying the whole table at the pin (8 bytes a page of the database, where the pin holds what changed).
- **Revisit if:** a measurement shows the turn-taking costs more than a quarter of the snapshot's speed, or the WAL growth under the held checkpoint is a problem in practice.
