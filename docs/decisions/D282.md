## D282. The fail-stop judge dates lag from the clock's lowest reading, and excuses a staged file a boot cannot place (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5) · Amends D081's list of the false-trip judge's exemptions

- **Question:** two nightly seeds failed with "fail-stop tripped with no cause: UnreplicatedTooLong". Was the engine early, or the judge too strict?
- **Clock step back:** a power loss kept two committed frames past the synced position, the clock then stepped back 14 s, the engine booted and first saw those frames, and the app committed 43 ms later. The engine dates frames by the observation that first saw them (`lsr_core::failstop`), so its clock started at the sighting, earlier on the wall clock than every commit's own stamp. The judge took the least commit stamp as the earliest date the engine could hold and called the trip 43 ms early. **Choice:** while lag runs, the judge's wall date is also lowered to each tick's start. A tick comes after the commits it is judged on, so its reading is a date the engine may legitimately hold; nothing changes unless the clock stepped back during the lag.
- **Unplaced staged file:** a boot after a generation change staged a snapshot equal to the replica, the store then refused conditional writes so it was never uploaded, and the process restarted. A restart forgets which pending files hold no commit; the boot finds one again only by comparing it with the staged file before it or, with none, by its WAL span ending where the replica's newest level-0 file's ends. A snapshot over a restarted WAL ends in another generation and stays counted, which #355 accepted. After a day-long clock step the engine tripped, correct by what it can know, false by what the simulator knows. **Choice:** a fourth exemption, counted as `failstop.excused.unplaced_file`: an age trip dated at the header stamp of the first pending staged file, while the judge sees the replica whole, when this life found the file in staging at its start, no staged file stands before it, and its WAL salts differ from those of the replica's file at the replica's newest transaction (or that file is gone). A boot that forgets a file it could place is still caught, and the swarm campaign reports the count.
- **Cost accepted:** a snapshot equal to the replica, over a restarted WAL, that a restart finds unuploaded and that stays unuploaded for the whole bound trips fail-stop. Go has no fail-stop to compare with. Not fixed in the engine: telling the snapshot equals the replica needs a whole-database checksum the replica may not carry (Go writes LTX files without one) or a restore, a request Go does not make.
- **Revisit if:** the engine gains a durable record of quiet files across a restart, or tracked checksums become owed.
