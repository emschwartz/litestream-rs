## D276. The simulator's gray weather: hangs, a slow staging disk, pauses inside a tick and a latency tail (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5) · test code only (`crates/sim`)

- **Question:** the simulator's stores answered at once, and every fault was an error or a wrong answer. Nothing tested the engine when a store is slow or silent but not failing, or when the process stops between two clock reads of one tick. What should the simulator draw, and with which numbers?
- **What Go does, and why:** not applicable to the engine. Go's S3 client has no request timeout of its own (24 hours in all); lsr's fails a request that makes no progress for 60 s (`crates/store-s3/src/config.rs` `DEFAULT_DEADLINE`, D235), as `StoreError::Transient` with `TimedOut` (`classify_transport`).
- **Choice** (fixed before the first run; changing any number is a new decision):
  1. **Time inside a tick** (`crates/sim/src/clock.rs` `Stall`): a hang or a pause moves every later clock read of the tick on, under either clock law. The driver's clock (which the stores and the recorders read) stays where the tick began and catches up when the tick settles, so the oracle's stamp bound still holds and the next tick begins after the stall.
  2. **A hang until the client's timeout** (`FaultShape::Hang`, `FaultShape::HangAfterPublish`, `HttpFault::Hang`): the call waits exactly the S3 client's default deadline, 60 s, read from `S3Config::deadline` (`crates/sim/src/remote.rs` `client_timeout`), then fails with the error the store's own `classify_transport` gives a transport timeout. `Hang` publishes nothing; `HangAfterPublish` (a quarter of the upload hangs) publishes and then times out. On the S3 replica half the hangs are the HTTP request itself timing out (`Fault::Transport` with `Timeout`), which never reaches the bucket.
  3. **An endpoint that hangs every call** (`Hold::Hang(op)`, shape `HangEvery`): 32 to 64 ticks, judged as every hold is, and by the hold's health judge: replication that halts through sixteen retries must leave a task degraded.
  4. **A slow staging disk** (`Hold::SlowStaging(factor)`): factor drawn uniform in 10 to 1000, for 2 to 11 ticks; each tick's staging calls are charged at the D265 staging price times the factor (latency times it, bandwidth divided by it). Nothing fails.
  5. **A pause inside a tick** (`Op::Pause`): before one of the tick's first eight clock reads after its own, the process stops for a third each of 1 s to 61 s, 1 min to 61 min, or 1 h to 48 h (`MAX_PAUSE_MS`), past the snapshot interval and the snapshot retention window (a day each).
  6. **Latency tail** (`Weights::latency_tail`, `crate::cost::tail_factor`): each replica request costs its D265 latency times a factor with P(factor ≥ k) = k^-1.5, cut at 1000: about 65 percent at the latency, 3 percent at ten times or more, 1 in 1000 at a hundred, 1 in 31,600 at the cap. Drawn from the run's own stream 43, one draw a request; a run with the tail is charged at the D265 prices plus the tail.
  7. **Drawing** (`crates/sim/src/generate.rs` `gray`): `Weights::gray`, a chance in a thousand that a step is weather, from stream 42, drawn at every step whatever a hold did, so the steps it does not replace are the steps of the same mix without it. A fifth each: a hang, a hang of every call, a slow disk, a pause. The swarm draws gray at 1 to 30 in a thousand in two thirds of its mixes and the latency tail in half, both last in the mix. **Off in recorded histories**: the default mix, every recorded history and every written scenario draw none, and `gray_weather_moves_no_step_it_does_not_replace` pins that the weather moves no other step.
  8. **Oracles:** every existing judge runs unchanged but one: the fail-stop judge dates a tick where its reads ended, not where it began, since a hung call's failure comes a timeout after the tick began (`an_age_trip_inside_a_hung_tick_is_dated_where_the_tick_ended`); the healthy tail runs after the weather has lifted (a slow disk and the latency tail are off in the tail), with the same lag and WAL bounds. Reach requires `gray.hang.calls`, `hold.hang.Upload.halted`, `gray.slow_staging.ticks`, `gray.pause.fired`, `gray.pause.past_a_day` and `gray.latency.tenfold` of the fixed seed set, and the soak's marks gate the new classes (`StoreFault.Hang`, `StoreFault.HangAfterPublish`, `Hold.Hang`, `Hold.SlowStaging`, `Pause`).
- **Cost accepted:** a hang's time is the client's whole timeout, though a real hung request also holds a connection, which the simulator does not price. The write-barrier judge (D269) is not on `main` yet, so nothing judges the barrier's wait against the slowed staging price; `Runner::profile_in_force` is the price it should be judged at when it lands. The latency tail is priced after the tick, as every D265 price is, so the engine never reads it inside the tick: only hangs and pauses move the clock mid-tick.
- **Revisit if:** the S3 client's default deadline changes, or a measurement of a real provider's latency gives another tail shape.
- **Links:** D265, D268, D269; plant `retry-waits-from-tick-start` (the gray test found the defect; the fix landed first, and the plant that mirrored it was retired).
