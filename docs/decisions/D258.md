## D258. An S3 upload that holds its parts says it is full before the write that would wait (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5)

- **Question:** a level-9 snapshot goes to the replica a slice a tick (D255) and asks its upload before each write whether the write would wait for a part. On S3 at a 100 MB part only one part is in flight (D232's 256 MiB budget), and the answer was "room" whenever no whole part waited. The write that then filled the part found no lane free, so the rest of that write waited on the engine's thread for the part in flight to end, about 6 s on Tigris from the studio. The engine synced and uploaded nothing of that database meanwhile. How does the upload answer?
- **What Go does, and why:** Go's snapshot is copied into the SDK's uploader (`s3/replica_client.go:513`, `manager.NewUploader`) on a goroutine of its own, so a part that waits blocks only that goroutine, and `Replica.monitor` keeps uploading level 0. lsr's engine is one thread, so the upload has to say so before the write instead.
- **Choice:** an upload that holds its parts (`S3Upload::write`) answers `Flight::Full` when every lane is taken and the part being filled has less than `ReadLen::CHUNK` (1 MiB) of room. `Flight::Room` now means a write of at most `ReadLen::CHUNK` does not wait; the snapshot writes at most 256 KiB and two pages at a time. The memory bound is unchanged: no part is added, the snapshot stops filling 1 MiB earlier.
- **Cost accepted:** at most the last mebibyte of each part is filled after the part before it ends, not during its upload.
- **Measured** (Mac Studio, macOS arm64, Tigris, 2 GiB database plus a 68 MiB one, 20 commits a second, levels 5s/10s/15s, `snapshot.interval` 1m, 100 MB parts, release builds, bench lock held; script not in the repository): the longest stretch in which the 2 GiB database's replicated transaction did not move, after the first snapshot, was 82 to 91 s at 63bebff51 (before D255), 16 to 25 s on main (three runs at 3dbeef6a2 and bd0da5531) and 6 s with this change. A file replica showed 2 to 3 s on both main and 63bebff51, since its snapshot takes 2 s. Sampling the process on main found the engine's thread in the snapshot's write, waiting for a part, for whole 2 s samples.
- **Revisit if:** the snapshot's writes grow past `ReadLen::CHUNK`, or the 1 MiB stop slows a snapshot measurably.
