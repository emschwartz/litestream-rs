## D250. Uploads are checked by their signed SHA-256, as in Go, in place of Content-MD5 and an ETag compare

Status: decided · Decided by: owner, 2026-11-25 · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5)

- **Question:** how the S3 store makes sure the bytes S3 keeps are the bytes it sent. It sent `Content-MD5` on every PUT and part, signed `UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD` (rusty-s3's only form), and compared each returned ETag with a local MD5. MD5 runs at about a third of SHA-256's speed on CPUs with SHA instructions (measured below), and the ETag compare is a check Go does not make.
- **Go:** signs every request's body (`SignPayload=true`, the SDK default) in `x-amz-content-sha256`, sends `Content-MD5` only on DeleteObjects (`RequireContentMD5`), not on PUT (`PutObjectContentMD5=false`), drops the SDK's checksum trailer for custom endpoints, and never compares an ETag.
- **Options shown:**
  1. Keep Content-MD5 and the ETag compare. Checks twice, costs the slower hash, and differs from Go.
  2. Sign the body's SHA-256 as Go does. One check, made by the store against a hash in the signature, on every provider that speaks SigV4.
  3. A CRC-32C checksum header. Faster still, but not every S3-compatible provider checks it, and Go does not send it.
  4. Nothing beyond TLS. TLS covers the wire, not the bytes between the read and the socket.
- **Choice:** option 2. Every request carries `x-amz-content-sha256` with the SHA-256 of its body, the empty-body hash when it has none, and signs it in the Authorization header (`crates/store-s3/src/sigv4.rs`, a small signer over `sha2`, tested on AWS's documented examples and RFC 4231's HMAC vectors). The hash is taken on the sending thread over a read checked cell by cell against the engine's read, as the MD5 was. `XAmzContentSHA256Mismatch` is read as `BadDigest` is, a checksum mismatch the engine answers with a new upload. `InvalidPart` on a completion (a part ETag the store does not hold) is read the same way, since the ETag is no longer checked when the part is answered.
- **What replaced each use of MD5:** the PUT and part `Content-MD5` header: the signed SHA-256. The ETag compare on a PUT or a part: nothing, as in Go; the store already refused a body that does not match its signed hash, and every read decodes the LTX file and checks its checksum. DeleteObjects keeps `Content-MD5`, which S3 requires there. The fake S3 of the tests still answers MD5 ETags, as S3 does.
- **Measured:** `sha2` picks SHA-NI on x86-64 and the SHA-2 extension on aarch64 at run time. Over 512 MiB in 256 KiB chunks, MD5 0.64 GB/s and SHA-256 2.05 GB/s on an Apple M-series laptop; the Linux box's numbers are in the pull request.
- **Cost accepted:** a hand-written SigV4 signer (about 200 lines with its tests) where rusty-s3's presigned URLs were used; rusty-s3 still builds the bucket URLs and the XML bodies.
- **Revisit if:** a supported provider refuses signed payloads, or a provider is found that answers 200 to a body other than the signed one.
