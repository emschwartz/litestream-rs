## D307. A checkpoint copies a held-back backlog with no write barrier held (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5)

- **Question:** a scheduled level-9 snapshot of a 24 GiB database held its read for 16 minutes, as Go's does, and no checkpoint could copy past it. The WAL reached 790 MB. The first checkpoint after it copied all of that inside the PASSIVE under the write barrier, and an application writer waited 5.9 s. Locally, snapshots of about two minutes gave waits of 3 to 7 s the same way. Must the copy happen under the barrier?
- **What Go does:** the same. `checkpoint` takes the barrier (`BEGIN; INSERT INTO _litestream_lock`), runs `verifyAndSync` and `PRAGMA wal_checkpoint(PASSIVE)`, then rolls back (`db.go`). The PASSIVE copies whatever its read lock held back while every writer waits. Go is wrong here: the barrier only has to keep writers out between the seal and the retake of the read, not during the copy.
- **Choice** (`crates/core/src/checkpoint.rs`): when more frames wait than `CheckpointConfig::barrier_frames` (twice the PASSIVE threshold, Go's 1000 when that trigger is off, never under 64), the checkpoint first takes a barrier of its own. Under it, it seals to the committed end and moves the read there (`Checkpointer::renew_read`: end the read, begin it again). It releases the barrier and runs a PASSIVE from the writer connection (`CheckpointMode::Backlog`), which copies everything below the moved read while the application writes on. The ordinary barrier, seal and PASSIVE then follow over what was committed meanwhile. A refused barrier or a copy refused because another connection holds the checkpoint lock is `LockBusy`: the caller asks again, since letting the barrier's PASSIVE go on would copy the whole backlog once that connection let go. A writer that restarted the WAL after the move is `Overtaken`, as for a refused bump.
- **The floor of 64:** under about a quarter of a MiB of 4 KiB pages the copy costs less than the extra barrier and read retake. It also keeps tests that set a PASSIVE threshold of one frame on the path they test.
- **Cost accepted:** one more barrier, seal and read retake on each checkpoint over a backlog: SQLite calls only, no store request. The application's own autocheckpoint can win the checkpoint lock after the move and pay the copy inside one of its commits; that is no worse than today. The database file's fsync now happens in the unlocked copy, so the barrier no longer waits for it on such a checkpoint.
- **The simulator** (this closes the gap D269 accepted): `crate::barrier` also counts the frames the engine's checkpoints copy while it holds the barrier, read from the model's `nBackfill` around each call. `run::tail` bounds them per barrier at the same rule (`2 * PASSIVE_PAGES`, at least 64) plus one commit made inside the tick. With the copy moved back under the barrier, 16 sim tests fail on it, among them seven of the eight fixed-seed groups over seeds 1 to 64; `barrier::a_backlog_a_reader_held_back_is_not_copied_under_the_barrier` is the explicit scenario.
