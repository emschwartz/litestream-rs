## D235. A request is bounded by stalls and by a cap that grows with the bytes it moves (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5)

One end-to-end deadline of 60 s bounded every request, a multipart part PUT included. A part that was still moving but needed more than 60 s failed as a timeout, was retried and failed the same way each time: a part of P bytes needs P/60 bytes a second on its connection. A first start or a snapshot of a large database could stop replicating on a link where Go keeps going ([#398](https://github.com/emschwartz/litestream-rs/issues/398)).

- **Question:** what bounds a request that moves slowly but steadily, without hanging on one that does not move?
- **Go:** a 24 hour client timeout over a whole request, a 30 s dial, and since benbjohnson/litestream#1635 a 30 s wait for the response head after the request is written (`ResponseHeaderTimeout`) and a 30 s deadline on each socket write, refreshed by every write (`s3/write_timeout.go`). Go's two new bounds measure lack of progress, not total time.
- **Choice:** the deadline (60 s, `DEFAULT_DEADLINE`; not Go's 30 s, which this change leaves alone) becomes a bound on lack of progress, and a cap on the whole request is added in `crates/store-s3/src/transport.rs` (`Bounds`). The cap is the deadline plus the time the bytes it moves need at 16 KiB a second: 380 s for Go's default 5 MiB part, about 68 minutes for a 64 MiB one. The bytes are the request body for an upload and, for a download, the response's declared `Content-Length` (for a ranged read, the range's length), counted from the first response byte; a request with no body and a small answer, a listing for one, is capped at the deadline plus the head wait plus a few seconds. The restore listing budget (D211) and the request bound D207 assume hold within that.
  - ureq (`crates/store-s3/src/ureq_transport.rs`): connect, each socket read, each socket write and the wait for the response head after the body is sent are each bounded by the deadline (`timeout_connect`, `timeout_per_read`, `timeout_per_write`, `timeout_recv_response`), the same three bounds Go has. The cap is a per-request `timeout_global`.
  - reqwest (`crates/store-s3/src/reqwest_transport.rs`): it has no bound on a stalled write, and its client-wide read timeout starts when the request does, so it would cut a long upload; an application's own client cannot be reconfigured either. The deadline bounds the connect on the store's own client, and the cap (a per-request `timeout`) is the only bound on the rest. A server that stops reading the body, or never answers after it, ends this transport at the cap, not at the deadline.
  - Downloads: ureq's per-read bound stays in force while the body is read, and `read_body` ends the read once it has taken longer than the cap of the length the server declared. reqwest bounds each chunk by the deadline and the whole body by the same cap, with a timer of its own around the send (the cap of the request body) and the read. A response with no declared length (chunked) has the deadline for its whole body; S3 and the stores here declare one.
  - Every read the store makes is one ranged GET through `Store::read` with a `ReadLen` of at most 16 MiB, so none is left on the 60 s cap: restore's file reads 4 MiB (316 s), compaction and snapshot source reads, the staged-file seed and witness reads 1 MiB (124 s), the level-0 read-back and its compare 256 KiB (76 s), the maintenance tail and the recovery header 17 and 100 bytes (60 s), the largest a `ReadLen` allows 16 MiB (1,084 s). A listing page or a delete answer is read the same way, by its declared length.
- **Cost accepted:** (1) A server that takes a body at any rate above 16 KiB a second and then never answers holds a reqwest request for the whole cap, 380 s for a 5 MiB part; under ureq it ends 60 s after the body was sent. (2) A server that stops reading holds a ureq part 60 s, as before, and a reqwest part up to its cap. (3) A connection that moves under 16 KiB a second is cut at the cap, as it was at 60 s: the floor is a number, not Go's none. (4) A download whose answer declares no length keeps the deadline for its body. (5) D211's "plus one request deadline" holds for a silent endpoint; a listing that trickles in answer may take its cap (the deadline plus its page's length at the floor, seconds for a page). (6) A delete or a listing with a small body gets a few seconds more.
- **Options considered:** Go's 24 hours with no floor (a peer that takes a byte a minute holds a part for a day, and a wedged store holds a drain past anything an operator waits for); a deadline that is a constant per part size (the cap tracks the body that was actually sent, with no second setting); a configurable deadline (a new user-visible setting waits for the owner); the 30 s of Go (the 60 s stays: a refused connection or a silent store takes as long to fail as before).
- **Revisit if:** a store takes a body quickly and answers slowly (a slow commit of a large part), so that 60 s without a head cuts a part Go would have waited for; a ranged read of a slow link trips its 60 s; or ureq gains a progress-based bound on the head wait that reqwest's client can share.
- **Tests:** `a_download_that_keeps_moving_outlives_the_stall_bound` on both transports (1 MiB in 16 KiB pieces 31 ms apart against a 1 s deadline; fails on the cap of an upload alone), and `ureq_transport::tests::an_upload_that_keeps_moving_outlives_the_stall_bound` and the same name in `reqwest_transport` (a server reading 4 MiB at about 1 MiB a second against a 3 s deadline; both fail on the end-to-end deadline), `a_body_the_server_never_reads_times_out_at_the_stall_bound` (ureq, 32 MiB, cap far past the time it took) and `a_body_the_server_never_reads_times_out_at_the_cap` (reqwest), `a_server_that_never_answers_times_out_at_the_stall_bound`, and `transport::tests::a_requests_cap_grows_with_its_body_at_the_floor`.
