## D235. A request is bounded by stalls and by a cap that grows with its body (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5)

One end-to-end deadline of 60 s bounded every request, a multipart part PUT included. A part that was still moving but needed more than 60 s failed as a timeout, was retried and failed the same way each time: a part of P bytes needs P/60 bytes a second on its connection. A first start or a snapshot of a large database could stop replicating on a link where Go keeps going ([#398](https://github.com/emschwartz/litestream-rs/issues/398)).

- **Question:** what bounds a request that moves slowly but steadily, without hanging on one that does not move?
- **Go:** a 24 hour client timeout over a whole request, a 30 s dial, and since benbjohnson/litestream#1635 a 30 s wait for the response head after the request is written (`ResponseHeaderTimeout`) and a 30 s deadline on each socket write, refreshed by every write (`s3/write_timeout.go`). Go's two new bounds measure lack of progress, not total time.
- **Choice:** the deadline (60 s, `DEFAULT_DEADLINE`; not Go's 30 s, which this change leaves alone) becomes a bound on lack of progress, and a cap on the whole request is added in `crates/store-s3/src/transport.rs` (`Bounds`). The cap is the deadline plus the time the request's body needs at 16 KiB a second: 380 s for Go's default 5 MiB part, about 68 minutes for a 64 MiB one. A request with no body is capped at the deadline, so a listing or a ranged read ends as it did, and the restore listing budget (D211) and the request bound D207 assume still hold word for word.
  - ureq (`crates/store-s3/src/ureq_transport.rs`): connect, each socket read, each socket write and the wait for the response head after the body is sent are each bounded by the deadline (`timeout_connect`, `timeout_per_read`, `timeout_per_write`, `timeout_recv_response`), the same three bounds Go has. The cap is a per-request `timeout_global`.
  - reqwest (`crates/store-s3/src/reqwest_transport.rs`): it has no bound on a stalled write, and its client-wide read timeout starts when the request does, so it would cut a long upload; an application's own client cannot be reconfigured either. The deadline bounds the connect on the store's own client, and the cap (a per-request `timeout`) is the only bound on the rest. A server that stops reading the body, or never answers after it, ends this transport at the cap, not at the deadline.
- **Cost accepted:** (1) A server that takes a body at any rate above 16 KiB a second and then never answers holds a reqwest request for the whole cap, 380 s for a 5 MiB part; under ureq it ends 60 s after the body was sent. (2) A server that stops reading holds a ureq part 60 s, as before, and a reqwest part up to its cap. (3) A connection that moves under 16 KiB a second is cut at the cap, as it was at 60 s: the floor is a number, not Go's none. (4) A body's length is the only thing that raises the cap: a ranged read that needs more than 60 s on a slow link still fails the same way, an old defect this change does not touch. (5) A delete or a listing with a small body gets a few seconds more.
- **Options considered:** Go's 24 hours with no floor (a peer that takes a byte a minute holds a part for a day, and a wedged store holds a drain past anything an operator waits for); a deadline that is a constant per part size (the cap tracks the body that was actually sent, with no second setting); a configurable deadline (a new user-visible setting waits for the owner); the 30 s of Go (the 60 s stays: a refused connection or a silent store takes as long to fail as before).
- **Revisit if:** a store takes a body quickly and answers slowly (a slow commit of a large part), so that 60 s without a head cuts a part Go would have waited for; a ranged read of a slow link trips its 60 s; or ureq gains a progress-based bound on the head wait that reqwest's client can share.
- **Tests:** `ureq_transport::tests::an_upload_that_keeps_moving_outlives_the_stall_bound` and the same name in `reqwest_transport` (a server reading 4 MiB at about 1 MiB a second against a 2 s deadline; both fail on the end-to-end deadline), `a_body_the_server_never_reads_times_out_at_the_stall_bound` (ureq, 32 MiB, cap far past the time it took) and `a_body_the_server_never_reads_times_out_at_the_cap` (reqwest), `a_server_that_never_answers_times_out_at_the_stall_bound`, and `transport::tests::a_requests_cap_grows_with_its_body_at_the_floor`.
