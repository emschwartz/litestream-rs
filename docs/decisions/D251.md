## D251. No cap on how many files a replica listing may hold in memory, as in Go

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5), delegated by the owner, 2026-11-25 · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5)

- **Question:** should the engine cap how many LTX files a replica listing may hold in memory, refuse past a cap, make it configurable, or stream listings instead (issue #68)?
- **Go:** no cap. Go pages each level's listing through an iterator, but its restore planner and its retention and compaction passes collect the files they keep into slices with no bound (`CalcRestorePlan` in `replica.go`, `compactor.go`).
- **Choice:** no cap, which is what runs today. The planner and retention read the inventory without copying it (tests `planning_a_hundred_thousand_files_copies_none_of_them` and the retention check in `crates/core/tests/core/mem.rs`), so memory is about 72 bytes a file: 0.7 MB at 10^4 files, 72 MB at 10^6. Default retention keeps a replica at hundreds to a few thousand files.
- **Cost accepted:** a replica with millions of files (retention switched off, or a very long window at one file a second) costs tens to hundreds of MB during a listing, and nothing refuses it. A hard or configurable cap would add a stop Go never makes for an ordinary replica.
- **Revisit if:** a replica past 10^6 files is seen in use. Streaming is scoped on issue #68 (about 4 to 5 thousand lines, the in-memory planner kept as its oracle).
