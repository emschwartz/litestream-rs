## D269. The simulator judges the application's wait on the write barrier (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5) · test code only (`crates/sim`)

- **Question:** every application writer waits while a checkpoint holds the write barrier. Nothing in the simulator measured that wait or checked that the barrier ended with its checkpoint, so a barrier kept across an upload, or one never released, passed every seed. What should be judged?
- **What Go does, and why:** Go takes the barrier (`BEGIN; INSERT INTO _litestream_lock`), runs `verifyAndSync` to the local level 0 and `PRAGMA wal_checkpoint`, and rolls back (`db.go` `checkpoint`). That is local work only.
- **Choice** (`crates/sim/src/barrier.rs`): a wrapper around the engine's checkpointer records both stores' counts while a barrier is held. After every tick the runner judges three things. There may be no replica request under a barrier. The wait, meaning store calls under the barrier priced at the D263 profile, may be at most one staged file per barrier: 4 requests and what one sync may stage. And when the tick ended without its process dying, no barrier may still be held. `barrier.judged` is a required mark.
- **Margin:** over the `sim` suite (9,501 barriers), no replica request was made under one, and the longest wait was 219 µs.
- **Cost accepted:** the price counts store calls only. SQLite's own copy of frames into the database during the PASSIVE is not priced, so a slow checkpoint inside SQLite is not seen.
- **Links:** D263; plant `barrier-left-held-after-checkpoint`.
