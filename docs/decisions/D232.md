## D232. An upload whose parts hold their bytes keeps them under 256 MiB (delegated; amends D226)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-fable-5-2, finished by claude-opus-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5)

- **Question:** an upload with no file behind it (a compaction's output, a level-9 snapshot) holds its parts in memory. D226 measured that as `concurrency x part size`: about a gigabyte at a large part size with ten parts at once, against about 720 MiB for Go at the same settings. An operator who moves from Go keeps the config file, so "set a smaller part size" is advice, not a fix. How many parts may such an upload hold?
- **What Go does, and why:** a streamed output goes through the AWS SDK's uploader, which takes each part from a pool of `Concurrency + 1` part buffers (`feature/s3/manager`, `upload.go`, `partPool`): the parts on the wire and one being filled. The pool has no byte bound, so its memory is `(concurrency + 1) x part size` whatever the part size.
- **Choice:** Go's shape, the parts in flight and one more, with a byte bound Go lacks. An upload that holds its parts has at most `min(concurrency, 256 MiB / part size - 1)` in flight, and never fewer than one (`held_width`, `crates/store-s3/src/part_plan.rs`). At the defaults (5 MiB, five at once) that is five in flight and 30 MiB held, so nothing changes. At a large part size with ten parts at once it is one in flight and one filling, two 100 MB parts. A part larger than half the budget still goes, one at a time, so the bound is then two parts. The upload of a staged file holds no part (D222) and keeps `concurrency` in flight.
- **Measured (branch claude/stream-budget, studio, macOS arm64, local MinIO, a whole first start of a 4 GiB database at a large part size with ten parts at once, peak physical footprint, n = 12 interleaved rounds):** 986.4 MiB with ten parts held; 213.9 MiB with two (-78.16% [-78.39, -77.93]); 413.1 MiB with four (-58.34% [-58.60, -58.07]); 508.9 MiB with five (-48.58% [-48.85, -48.31]); A/A +0.09% [-0.27, +0.44]. That run's wall arm failed its A/A gate (the box was loaded) and is not a result. The wall and memory of this change against the code before it are in D233.
- **Cost accepted:** at a large part size a compaction or snapshot sends one part at a time instead of ten. On a fast link that can lengthen the streamed uploads; the staged file, which is most of a first start's bytes, keeps all ten.
- **Options considered:** Go's `(concurrency + 1) x part` with no byte bound (about 1.1 GB at a large part size with ten at once, more than Go's measured peak); 384 or 512 MiB (four or five parts at a large part size; the measured memory above, no wall result that would justify the extra); a spool file for streamed outputs so they could be read twice like a staged file (a file Go does not write).
- **Revisit if:** a quiet-box measurement shows the streamed uploads at a large part size cost more than a quarter of a first start's wall time with the budget at 256 MiB against 512 MiB.
- **Links:** D226, D208, D222, D233; `crates/store-s3/src/part_plan.rs` (`HELD_BUDGET`, `held_width`), `crates/store-s3/src/upload.rs`, `docs/planted-bugs/entries/held-budget-ignored.toml`.
