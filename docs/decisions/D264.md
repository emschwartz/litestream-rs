## D264. A job's slices and a level-0 file's share one count ahead of the WAL step, and take turns inside it (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-opus-5-5) · Fixes the turn order of docs/decisions/D255.md and docs/decisions/D259.md

- **Question:** a job part-way (a level-9 snapshot or a compaction, sent a slice a tick) and a level-0 file in flight each counted their own slices. After the job's eight slices the tick owed to the rest of the engine went to the file's slice, whose own count of eight had barely moved, so the WAL step waited nine slices or more (up to 72) while a commit was waiting. The simulator's liveness tail (branch `claude/sim-liveness`, its D263 finding) reported "slices in a row ahead of a waiting commit: 9 is over the limit 8". How do the two share the ticks?
- **What Go does, and why:** Go runs the sync (`DB.monitor`), the level-0 upload (`Replica.monitor`) and the snapshot or compaction on goroutines of their own, so a commit is synced on the next poll whatever is being sent, and the snapshot and the uploads share the bandwidth. The single-threaded actor approximates that with turns.
- **Choice:**
  1. One count of slices ahead of the WAL step (`slices_ahead`), raised by a job's slice and a file's alike. At eight (`FAIR_SHARE`) neither goes; the WAL step is offered the tick, and the count starts again whenever it is offered one, taken or not.
  2. Inside those eight the job and an upload with work take turns: after a job's slice the upload goes next (`job_went_last`), whether a file is in flight or only staged. A job no longer goes first for its whole share while an upload waits.
- **Cost accepted:**
  - With a large level-0 file in flight, the snapshot gets half the slices instead of eight of nine, so it takes longer by up to the file's slices. The file goes up faster by the same count.
  - One more bool in the engine; the job's own counter is gone.
- **Measured** (Mac Studio, macOS arm64, internal disk, directory replica; main at 6a37978e2 against this branch, release builds; the D255 setup: a 4 GiB database under 20 commits a second, the scheduled snapshot read from the database with `snapshot.interval` at 1 minute, bench lock held, arms interleaved; the script is not in the repository):
  - 16 KiB commits, three runs each: the longest stretch with no level-0 upload from the first snapshot to 3 s after the scheduled one is 1.095 to 1.103 s on main and 1.085 to 1.094 s here, the one-second poll interval; the snapshot takes 2.30 to 2.34 s and 2.29 to 2.51 s in about 510 slices. Level-0 files here are a fraction of a slice, so none is in flight beside the snapshot; the fix only moves a staged file's upload to the tick after the job's next slice, milliseconds sooner.
  - 1 MiB commits (level-0 files of about 20 MiB, three slices each), two runs each: 1.19 to 1.22 s on main and 1.16 to 1.21 s here. The real stall the simulator found needs a file in flight while a commit waits, and at 8 MiB slices it is a few slices long, tens of milliseconds, below what a one-second poll shows.
- **Options considered:** counting the job's slices with the file's but letting the job go first for the whole window (the WAL step keeps its bound, but a waiting upload has only the ticks the WAL step leaves, none while commits arrive every tick); letting the WAL step take a turn after every slice (a sync and a level-0 file per slice under steady commits, the cost D217 avoided).
- **Revisit if:** the simulator or a measurement shows a snapshot taking more than twice as long while level-0 files are in flight.
