## D297. Each swarm-reachable plant carries an unaided budget or a stated miss (delegated)

Status: decided · Decided by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5), **delegated**; the owner can overturn it · Recorded by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5) · test and tooling only (`scripts/planted-bugs.py`, the justfile, the nightly workflow, the entry files)

- **Question:** `just planted-bugs-unaided` printed seeds to the first failure per plant and judged nothing, so a plant the swarm stopped finding went unnoticed. What must a run judge?
- **Choice:** every non-canary entry of `lsr-sim` and `lsr-core` (259 entries, 224 distinct patches) carries `unaided = <seeds>` (the swarm must fail within that many seeds from base 1,000,001) or `unaided_miss = "<why>"`. `just planted-bugs-names` (in `just check`) refuses an entry with neither, with both, or whose entries that share a patch disagree. `--unaided` fails on a budgeted plant found later or never, and on a marked miss the swarm finds within `--seeds` (default 100): the miss is stale and its sentence is false. A plant whose first failure is the clean tree's own is reported as masked and fails too. `just planted-bugs-judge-test` (in `just check`) runs the judge over fake rows.
- **Budget rule, fixed before the judging run:** budget = min(300, max(4 x measured, 50)), where measured is the seeds to first failure in the measuring run. The cap is that run's seed count, so a plant first found past seed 75 gets 300, which is headroom of under 4x.
- **Measuring run (registration 1):** `--unaided` with 300 seeds per plant from base 1,000,001 at 4918e715e, over the 219 patches then in scope: 46 failed within 300 seeds and 173 did not. Measured seeds to first failure, sorted: 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 4, 4, 4, 5, 6, 6, 6, 6, 6, 7, 8, 8, 13, 17, 17, 18, 18, 18, 21, 26, 29, 29, 29, 30, 33, 44, 63, 73, 76, 79, 86, 151, 200, 206, 231, 249, 258, 258, 263. Nine plants added by main afterwards (published-file-takes-no-turn, read-back-reads-whole-files, read-back-skips-the-tail, refused-output-deletes-the-key, refused-output-owes-no-read-back, rejected-write-deletes-the-key, retry-waits-from-tick-start, settle-course-lists-again, upload-forgets-a-wiped-staging) were measured the same way at 6db4f4217: none failed in 300 seeds. The generator was not changed.
- **Judging run:** the same deterministic base (1,000,001), at the head that lands, in three parallel shards. The swarm is deterministic per seed, so this is a regression gate (it fails when a swarm change, a generator change or a patch drift moves a first failure past its budget), not a fresh estimate of recall. A fresh base would measure recall again, but 4x headroom would then flap on seeds the measuring run never saw; recall on a second base is a separate registration. A miss is judged on its first 100 seeds only: the 300-seed run found nothing in them, so a failure there means the swarm changed. Result at head: 255 of 259 entries judged as recorded; 4 entries (2 patches) failed, see the finding.
- **Finding, a recall regression between the two runs:** boot-gate-refuses-nothing (first failure at seed 249, budget 300) and clock-step-back-freezes-timers (seed 4, budget 50), each with its `-sim` entry, were not found in their budget at f6c379c5d. Nine commits of main separate the runs (#488, #494 to #501); #495 (a retry's wait timed from when the failed call returned) is the likeliest for the clock plant, and none was bisected. The clock-step-back plant, found at seed 4, was re-run at 300 seeds at the head and failed in none. Both are marked misses with that history in the sentence; the stale check will report them if the swarm finds them again. This is the judge doing its job, and a sign the swarm's recall for such plants depends on one draw, not on a generator that reaches the state on purpose.
- **Misses:** the 200 entries marked `unaided_miss` (180 patches) carry one sentence each, ten sentences over seven classes (patches per sentence at the measuring run: 13, 31, 49, 7, 26, 20, 23, 2, 1, 1), plus one for each of the nine new plants and the two lost ones. A sentence is a classification read from the entry's own comment and its named tests, not a trace of why the seeds pass: the stale check keeps it from drifting one way, and a sentence proved wrong by a trace is rewritten. The classes: fail-stop lag that only a named fail-stop scenario holds long enough; cost plants (requests, memory, bytes) that change no restored page; compaction or listing state that needs a backlog or a hole; the local course; single decision points needing one precise fault; plants in the simulator's own judges; interleavings at one request or tick.
- **Cost, measured:** 500 seeds of the clean tree took 290 s (0.58 s a seed at load average 14); 200 seeds of one plant with its build took 153 s. The judging run took about one hour of wall time on three shards of a loaded machine. Budgeted plants run their budget (5,812 seeds in total), misses run 100 (18,000), and each shard runs the clean tree once for up to 300: about 26,000 seeds and 224 builds, estimated at 25 to 30 minutes a shard in eight shards (about 220 runner-minutes a night, ubuntu-latest). The nightly job `planted-bugs-unaided` has a 60-minute limit; the first nightly calibrates the estimate. Misses at 300 seeds would cost about 54,000 seeds, over 60 minutes a shard.
- **Also changed:** the swarm runs 25 seeds per nextest invocation (was 100): at 100 a loaded box passed the 90 s per-test limit, which showed as `ERROR` with no first-failure line (24 of 219 plants in the first measuring pass; rerun with the new chunk).
- **Cost accepted:** a budget with 4x headroom detects a swarm that got much worse, not one that got somewhat worse (a first failure moving from 6 to 40 passes), and a plant found by one lucky seed (like the one at 249) flaps with any change to the swarm's draw. The miss sentences are hypotheses until a generator change tests them.
- **Revisit if:** the generator changes (re-measure and rewrite every budget in one commit), a shard nears 40 minutes, or the stale-miss check fires (set the budget from that run).
