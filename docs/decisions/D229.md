## D229. LZ4 backend: stay on lz4_flex's safe mode (decided)

Status: decided · Decided by: owner, 2026-11-25 · Recorded by: Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5)

The owner's words: "I'm interested to know whether the faster LZ4 mode makes much of a difference but we can stay on the safe one for now."

- **Question:** does a faster LZ4 (lz4_flex's unsafe code, or liblz4 through lzzzz) pay for what it costs?
- **Choice:** keep lz4_flex on its safe features. No change to `Cargo.toml`.
- **Measured, Apple M1 Max (2 GiB databases, 12 interleaved pairs):** the unsafe encoder compresses 4 to 9% faster in the micro-benchmark. The unsafe decoder gains nothing measurable. liblz4 compresses about like the unsafe encoder and decodes text-like pages 50% faster, zero pages 10x. End to end the first start's CPU moves by 3 to 5% (text) and under 3% (random), wall under 3%, restore within 1%. Compressed size is within 0.1% in every arm.
- **Measured, x86:** compression is 14 to 22% faster with the unsafe encoder. The hybrid (unsafe encoder, safe decoder, features `["std", "safe-decode"]`) keeps all of that gain: the unsafe decoder is no faster and slower on zero pages. End to end on a first start, about 4% less CPU on text-like data; not resolved on random data.
- **Also found:** lz4_flex's `compress_into` allocates a zeroed hash table on every call (`hashtable.rs:73`) and offers no way to reuse it, about 1% of CPU.
- **Not chosen:** the hybrid for now (unsafe code in lz4_flex's compress.rs, sink.rs and fastcpy_unsafe.rs that is not ours, under the workspace's `unsafe` denial only through a dependency); liblz4 (a C build in every target, two tests that pin lz4_flex's exact bytes would change, and its tighter output bound would change what the tolerant reader accepts, frame.rs `get_maximum_output_size`).
- **Cost accepted:** compression stays 14 to 22% slower on x86 than it could be, about 4% more CPU on a first start of text-like data.
- **Revisit when:** a formally checked unsafe version of lz4_flex exists (another session is proposing one; the issue will be linked here once it exists), or a first start becomes CPU bound enough that 4% matters, or a decoder-heavy workload appears (then liblz4).
